# Program Blueprint

## Product definition

STEAM PNKS is one integrated experience with four mutually reinforcing parts:

1. **Crew** creates belonging, shared norms, peer support, and youth leadership.
2. **Missions** create meaningful reasons to learn and make.
3. **Studio** provides tools, materials, mentoring, and protected time.
4. **Ranks** recognize demonstrated growth, craft, contribution, and reflection.

Courses unlock missions. The studio enables missions. Crew relationships sustain missions. Ranks make growth visible. The hangout quality comes from unhurried social time inside a well-designed, supervised program—not from an unstructured drop-in promise.

## Founding cohort hypothesis

**Assumption to test:** ages 12–15, 12–16 participants, six weekly sessions, one qualified partner venue, two or more screened adults subject to local requirements.

This range is chosen to make the first design concrete. It is not a verified statement about demand or a substitute for local safety and staffing rules.

## Member experience

Each session follows a predictable rhythm:

| Phase | Purpose | Typical time |
|---|---|---:|
| Arrive + connect | Snack, check-in, informal connection, preview | 15 min |
| Skill spark | Short demonstration tied to the mission | 20 min |
| Make | Build, test, document, and seek help | 70 min |
| Share + help | Peer critique, troubleshooting, contribution | 20 min |
| Reflect + reset | Portfolio note, cleanup, checkout | 15 min |

Exact duration is a pilot variable. Accessibility, sensory, medical, religious, transport, and fatigue needs may require different rhythms.

## Prototype ranks

Ranks represent identity growth, not tool permission. Tool certifications remain separate and site-specific.

| Rank | Meaning | Evidence pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **Spark** | I can enter the maker community safely and turn curiosity into a first artifact. | One completed build; safety habits; reflection; useful peer contribution |
| **Builder** | I can plan, iterate, combine skills, and improve work through feedback. | Two or more portfolio artifacts; documented iteration; skill evidence; peer support |
| **Catalyst** | I can lead responsibly and use making to create value beyond myself. | Sustained project; user/community input; leadership; impact evidence; public reflection |

Advancement rules:

- Require multiple forms of evidence: artifact, observation, explanation, and reflection.
- Allow multiple media for demonstration; writing fluency must not become a hidden gate.
- Use transparent rubrics and calibration among adults.
- Never rank popularity, speed, expensive materials, or prior access.
- Permit revision and appeal.
- Award recognition in a way that preserves dignity and does not publicly expose a member’s support needs.

## Achievement taxonomy

Badges should be limited, legible, and evidence-based.

- **Practice:** design, coding, electronics, fabrication, media, science, repair.
- **Craft:** precision, reliability, finish, aesthetics, documentation.
- **Community:** mentoring, collaboration, stewardship, welcome, teaching.
- **Impact:** investigated need, user testing, accessibility, repair, civic/environmental value.
- **Wildcards:** playful experiments, unusual combinations, ambitious failures with excellent learning.

The founding pilot should test no more than six achievements. Do not build a large badge catalog before observing what participants actually value.

## Learning principles

- Start with a meaningful problem or expressive possibility, not a tool tour.
- Teach skills just in time, then require application.
- Treat aesthetics and technical performance as complementary.
- Make iteration visible and celebrate well-examined failure.
- Provide real choice inside safe constraints.
- Design for collaboration without making extroversion a requirement.
- Include repair, reuse, and environmental tradeoffs.
- Separate AI assistance from evidence of the member’s own understanding.

## Responsible AI strand

Young people should learn to use AI as a fallible creative and technical collaborator.

- Label what was generated, retrieved, inferred, or personally created.
- Verify consequential facts against reliable sources.
- Do not submit personal, sensitive, copyrighted, or partner-confidential material without permission.
- Test generated code and designs before use.
- Examine bias, persuasive design, environmental cost, and labor implications.
- Keep a non-AI pathway available without loss of rank or status.

## Inclusion by design

- Offer visual, spoken, written, and hands-on instruction.
- Publish physical, sensory, communication, and tool demands before enrollment.
- Provide an accommodations request route managed by a qualified person.
- Budget for adaptive tools, quiet space, transport support, and scholarships.
- Audit who applies, enrolls, attends, advances, leads, and leaves.
- Never use “fit” or “independence” as an unexamined exclusion criterion.
