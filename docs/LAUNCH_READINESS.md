# Launch Readiness

**Purpose:** turn a compelling concept into a company that can responsibly test, learn, and earn trust.

**Current status:** concept validated only at the narrative level. No local demand, partner, team, safety system, business model, or name clearance is yet documented as complete.

## Launch thesis

STEAM PNKS should launch first as a tightly scoped, partner-hosted pilot—not as a permanent makerspace. This preserves the essential concept while reducing fixed cost, operational complexity, and safety exposure. The company earns the right to expand by producing evidence of belonging, skill growth, safe delivery, parent trust, and sustainable economics.

## The offer

> A project crew for young makers who want to build real things, develop practical skills, find their people, and make something that matters.

The initial customer and beneficiary are different:

- **Young people** choose identity, agency, creativity, friends, and access to tools.
- **Families** choose safe adults, visible growth, belonging, logistics, and value.
- **Host partners** choose a distinctive youth experience with clear staffing, risk controls, and measurable outcomes.

## Stage gates

| Gate | Evidence required | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Founder scope | Geography, age cohort, business intent, budget, time, and pilot window | **DAN NEEDED** |
| 2. Name viability | Professional trademark/name/domain review in intended markets | **DAN NEEDED** |
| 3. Local demand | Structured youth, parent, educator, and partner discovery | Not started |
| 4. Qualified ownership | Named safeguarding, program, and operations leads with suitable credentials | **DAN NEEDED** |
| 5. Safe delivery | Approved policies, screening, insurance, site, tools, emergency plan, and accommodations | Not started |
| 6. Pilot economics | Budget, price/access model, scholarships, staffing load, and downside limit | Not started |
| 7. Pilot readiness | Curriculum rehearsal, consent, communications, data minimization, and go/no-go review | Not started |
| 8. Public launch | Approved facts, contact path, privacy terms, accessibility, and evidence-based claims | Not started |

No later gate overrides an incomplete safety or legal gate.

## 90-day sequence

### Days 1–14 — define and de-risk

- Complete founder decisions and set a maximum pilot downside.
- Brief qualified trademark counsel on exact and similar names.
- Appoint qualified safeguarding and operations owners.
- Identify three potential partner venues and map local requirements.
- Open decision, risk, source, and interview logs.

### Days 8–28 — discover

- Interview at least 12 young people, 12 parents/caregivers, and five educators or potential partners.
- Test the promise, schedule, transport, price/access expectations, and concerns.
- Observe or visit comparable programs without misrepresenting the company.
- Summarize patterns; do not cherry-pick enthusiastic quotes.

### Days 22–42 — design

- Select one partner-site hypothesis and one six-session schedule.
- Complete the pilot curriculum, materials list, staffing plan, accommodations process, and evaluation design.
- Complete the local safeguarding, workshop, insurance, privacy, and emergency requirements.
- Price both the true delivery cost and an accessible participant contribution.

### Days 43–78 — recruit and deliver

- Recruit only after the readiness review is signed.
- Use clear consent, collection/pickup, photo, communications, and incident rules.
- Run staff rehearsal before session one.
- Capture attendance, safe-delivery events, portfolio evidence, belonging pulse, and delivery effort.

### Days 79–90 — decide

- Review youth, family, staff, and partner feedback together.
- Compare outcomes and delivery burden against thresholds defined before the pilot.
- Decide: continue, revise, partner/license, pause, rename, or stop.
- Publish an honest internal pilot report; publish external claims only with consent and traceable evidence.

## Company operating roles

One person may hold multiple roles early, but every responsibility must have one named owner.

| Role | Accountable for | Human approval required |
|---|---|---|
| Founder/venture lead | Purpose, capital, structure, partnerships, final go/no-go | All irreversible commitments |
| Safeguarding lead | Youth protection, reporting, boundaries, screening, training | All youth-facing activity |
| Program lead | Learning design, facilitation, mentors, inclusion | Pilot curriculum and delivery |
| Operations lead | Site, insurance, emergency readiness, tools, materials, logistics | Site and session readiness |
| Research/evaluation lead | Consent, instruments, evidence quality, reporting | Claims and participant research |
| Brand/release lead | Website, communications, accessibility, provenance | Public release |

AI may draft, organize, analyze, challenge, and quality-check. It may not approve safeguarding, legal clearance, insurance, participant eligibility, incident handling, or public claims about real outcomes.

## Business-model experiments

Test these in order of reversibility:

1. Sponsored or grant-supported partner pilot.
2. Family contribution with need-blind scholarship access.
3. Partner-paid cohort for schools, libraries, museums, or community organizations.
4. Repeat membership only after retention and delivery capacity are understood.
5. Curriculum licensing or permanent premises only after multiple successful cohorts.

## DAN NEEDED — founder brief

- Intended city, state/province, and country.
- Local program, venture-backed company, nonprofit, social enterprise, licensing platform, or hybrid intent.
- Founder time available each week and desired personal operating role.
- Maximum discovery and pilot budget; acceptable financial downside.
- Earliest responsible pilot window.
- Willingness to rename if clearance or audience research points elsewhere.
- Existing relationships with youth organizations, educators, venues, insurers, or funders.
- Names and credentials of proposed safeguarding and operations owners.

Until these are answered, the current materials are a launch-ready framework—not a representation that the company is legally or operationally ready to enroll.
