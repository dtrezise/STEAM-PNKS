# Safety and Safeguarding Framework

**Status:** readiness framework only. It is not a complete policy set, legal determination, training program, risk assessment, or authorization to serve young people.

STEAM PNKS combines youth programming, social space, and tools. That combination requires both child safeguarding and workshop safety. Excitement, waivers, parent presence, or a host venue do not replace qualified ownership.

## Required accountable owners

- **DAN NEEDED — Safeguarding lead:** qualified for the launch jurisdiction and population.
- **DAN NEEDED — Operations/site safety lead:** competent for the venue, tools, materials, and emergency system.
- **DAN NEEDED — Insurance/legal contacts:** able to confirm entity, staffing, venue, activity, transport, data, and reporting obligations.

## Readiness domains

| Domain | Minimum deliverables before recruitment |
|---|---|
| Safeguarding | Code of conduct; boundaries; screening; training; supervision/visibility rules; concern reporting; mandatory-reporting procedure; retaliation protection |
| People | Role descriptions; qualification checks; references where appropriate; staffing and backup plan; youth leadership boundaries; visitor rules |
| Enrollment | Eligibility; informed caregiver consent and youth assent; medical/emergency data minimization; accommodations; authorized pickup; attendance controls |
| Communication | Approved channels; no private adult-youth messaging; hours and response rules; records; caregiver visibility; social media boundaries |
| Site | Access control; sightlines; toilets/changing; accessibility; emergency exits; first aid; fire/severe weather; storage; cleanup; food/allergen controls |
| Tools/materials | Inventory; task-specific risk assessments; training; PPE; ventilation; maintenance; lockout/storage; competency authorization; stop-work rule |
| Digital/data | Least data necessary; role-based access; retention/deletion; photo/media choice; device/account rules; breach response; no sensitive data in consumer AI tools |
| Incident response | Immediate care; escalation tree; documentation; caregiver notification; external reporting; preservation of evidence; aftercare; review and corrective action |
| Transport/off-site | Explicit ownership; handoff; vehicles/drivers; ratios; emergency communication; accessibility; excursion risk review |
| Inclusion | Accommodations process; confidential handling; adaptive equipment; sensory/quiet options; behavior support; non-discrimination; appeal route |

## Workshop control hierarchy

Use controls in this order:

1. Eliminate the hazardous activity from the first pilot where it is not necessary to test the concept.
2. Substitute lower-risk tools, materials, and processes.
3. Use engineering controls such as guarding, containment, and appropriate ventilation.
4. Use administrative controls: access levels, training, supervision, setup, signage, maintenance, and scheduling.
5. Use correctly selected protective equipment as the final layer—not the whole safety system.

Tool certification is specific to the person, tool, task, site, and supervision condition. A rank or badge never grants universal permission.

## Session go/no-go

The named session lead records the result before doors open:

- Correct adults present, screened/trained as required, with backup plan.
- Participant roster, contacts, accommodations, permissions, and collection plan available only to those who need them.
- Site walk complete; exits, first aid, communication, and emergency access clear.
- Tools/materials inspected and access-controlled.
- Ventilation and protective controls appropriate for planned work.
- Weather, illness, facility, and transport conditions acceptable.
- Staff brief complete, including boundaries, individual needs, and stop-work authority.

Any accountable lead may stop or modify a session without penalty.

## Safeguarding culture

- Design for observable interactions and shared accountability.
- Make it easy for a young person to seek help from more than one trusted adult.
- Teach rights and reporting routes in age-appropriate language.
- Do not promise confidentiality that cannot be kept.
- Separate behavior support from shame, exclusion, or public ranking.
- Treat near misses, ambiguity, and discomfort as learning signals.
- Review power imbalances in mentoring, awards, photography, transportation, and digital contact.

## External review required

Before enrollment, qualified local reviewers must resolve:

- entity, licensing, background-check, mandatory-reporting, labor/volunteer, accessibility, privacy, and recordkeeping requirements;
- insurance coverage and exclusions;
- venue-specific occupancy, fire, health, building, and workshop requirements;
- medical, emergency, and incident processes;
- whether any activity or equipment is appropriate for the selected ages and staffing.

The final approved policies should include version, owner, approval date, review date, training record, and change log.
